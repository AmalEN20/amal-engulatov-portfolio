# Home hero motion contract

1. **Narrative purpose:** identify Amal through one semantic `Amal Engulatov` heading whose visible editorial units are `Amal` and `ENG`, optically balanced at an equal offset from the centered, waist-up ASCII portrait. The name uses a very light Geist cut with protected mask padding. A light, borderless two-sided metadata object follows `ENG` and uses only verified geography (`Seattle, Washington / New York`) and focus areas (`Frontend Development / Product · AI`). The scene resolves into the actual Home `Work` section.
2. **Entry:** `Amal` and `ENG` rise from separate clipped masks with a restrained stagger. The centered portrait keeps its approved upward clip reveal. The metadata object enters after `ENG` and begins its time-controlled face/value cycle only in the resting state. The shared SiteShell remains the owner of first-load and route-transition timing.
3. **Scroll interval:** the stage pins across a 270svh desktop wrapper. From 0–10% the complete portrait, split name, and metadata remain fixed and legible. Glyphs then follow independently seeded curved directions from 10–60%; every ray is calculated to cross a viewport edge. A larger deterministic subset of those same portrait glyphs is bent through a dense low-discrepancy distribution across all three protected text regions, without changing each particle's final exit direction. The distribution covers the full ink field rather than clustering around a few random points. While an existing portrait glyph crosses `Amal`, `ENG`, or metadata, its widened fully opaque white trail is clipped to that region and removes the ink immediately. The clip carries a restrained 1.5px bleed beyond the protected bounds so the trail covers the text edge without exposing antialiased letter residue. Protected geometry is refreshed while the scatter is active, so the metadata target remains current after its entry transform and during its rotor/value loop instead of erasing against an early stale rectangle. Trail geometry uses fixed samples along the particle's actual curve, so an already erased line never shifts, fades, or passes through a gray phase as progress advances. The natural trails are composited before the moving glyph layer: the ink disappears, but source particles remain visible above their paths and continue offscreen instead of being cut into rectangular white holes. There is no late cleanup cohort, generated glyph head, rectangle fill, binary seal, DOM opacity wash, or independent disappearance: only particles that visibly leave the portrait perform the erasure, and their wider overlapping paths complete it before the DOM identity is hidden at 60%. From 60–68% the stage holds a deliberately empty white frame. `Work` rises from a line mask from 68–82% and remains as the resting scene through the end.
4. **Exit, reverse, and navbar signal:** every page-local transform and erase trail is derived from the same normalized progress, so reverse scroll reconstructs the exact composition and restores the text along the particle paths. Home owns `html[data-home-hero]`: `intro` at rest, `exiting` after 5%, and `cleared` only at or beyond 60.5%, immediately after the large identity and metadata have fully disappeared; reverse returns to `exiting/intro`, and unmount deletes the attribute. Portfolio Main owns the matching shared navbar reveal and its lighter brand weight. `Amal` is the only Home control in the navbar; there is no duplicate `Home` link. Route exit remains owned by the shared state machine.
5. **Layer ownership:** DOM/CSS own semantic text, protected erase-target masks, the cardless metadata turn, the balanced identity positions, the empty beat, and the Work reveal. The existing full-viewport decorative Canvas is portaled directly to `body` and owns the high-count glyph trajectories, white clipped erase trails, ambient drift, pointer displacement, and graphite halo. It stays below navbar at rest, rises alone above navbar while particles travel, never accepts pointer input, and returns below navbar once erasure completes. The Home passive listener reads scroll emitted by the shared SiteShell owner, writes CSS progress, and updates a ref consumed by the existing portrait RAF. It creates no Lenis instance, scroll RAF, WebGL context, video, or animation-library timeline.
6. **Variants:** desktop places `Amal` and `ENG` on opposing sides of the portrait. Mobile keeps the same order with smaller light type, a shorter 220svh wrapper, native touch, and capped glyph density. Reduced motion renders two ordinary 100svh sections: a static complete hero followed by immediately visible Work; it reports `data-home-hero="cleared"` so Main can show the navbar brand without decorative delay.
7. **Budget and cleanup:** the portrait source remains about 36 KB and is used only as the hidden Canvas sampling input; the photographic `<Image>` fallback never becomes visually visible, preventing a route-mount flash of the source photograph. Canvas DPR is capped at 1.5 desktop and 1.15 touch, rendering remains capped near 30 fps, and the loop stops offscreen. Exit vectors are calculated only when the portrait is sampled and reuse the existing Canvas loop. RAF, observers, pointer/media listeners, source listener, and `data-home-hero` clean up on unmount.
8. **Acceptance:** the complete accessible name remains intact while visible text reads `Amal / ENG`; the two name units have equal optical distance from the figure; metadata follows `ENG`; the source photograph never flashes before Canvas readiness; the name and metadata do not translate, fade, or receive any opacity animation during scatter; every crossed trail is opaque white on first contact and its already drawn prefix remains geometrically fixed; existing portrait glyphs and their wider overlapping paths—not a late cleanup cohort, generated heads, fill, or DOM hide—remove all identity and metadata ink before the 60% boundary; natural departing glyphs may remain temporarily visible over the protected bounds, but no rectangular white holes or letter-shaped residue may remain; glyphs still take varied curved paths and travel above navbar content; reverse progress restores the same erased paths without snapping; 60–68% is an empty stopped frame; Work starts at 68% and is fully readable by 82%; the only Home control is the `Amal` brand, which stays hidden through identity exit and switches to `cleared` at 60.5%, reverses correctly, and deletes on unmount; cursor response still eases; 390px and 430px have no horizontal overflow; reduced motion has no pin, scatter, metadata loop, cursor loop, or erase travel; no console errors; lint and build pass.
